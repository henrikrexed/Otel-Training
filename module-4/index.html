    M4 — Sampling: cost/fidelity strategy

    Goal: cut ingest cost without losing the traces you would actually debug. Break it naively first, then build a defensible policy on the collector. 30 minutes.

    Concepts (5 min)

    Two places to sample, two trade-offs:

    • Head sampling — decided at span creation, in the SDK (parent_based + traceidratio). Nearly free, but blind: the decision is made before you know whether the request fails or drags.
    • Tail sampling — decided when the trace completes, in the collector (tail_sampling processor). Informed: it has seen the errors and the durations. Expensive: the collector buffers every trace until decision time.

    The pattern that survives contact with production: head sampling generous (usually always_on in-cluster), tail sampling smart at the aggregation tier — keep all errors, keep all slow traces, sample the rest — plus the spanmetrics connector computing RED metrics (rate / errors / duration) from every span before sampling drops anything, so your dashboards survive aggressive sampling.

    Step 1 — baseline: measure before you touch anything

    The k6 load generator drives continuous traffic. In Dynatrace, Distributed traces, your cluster filter, last 15 minutes. Record:

    • trace count: ______
    • traces with status = ERROR (add the filter): ______
    • rough p95 of a game-service endpoint (e.g. from the response-time distribution): ______

    This is the fidelity baseline. Every change below is judged against it.

    Step 2 — break it: naive head sampling

    If you sampled 10% at the head you would save ~90% of span ingest. Try it on one service. In Gitea, 33-blackjack.yaml, add to the container env:

    - name: OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER
  value: "parentbased_traceidratio"
- name: OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER_ARG
  value: "0.1"

    Commit, wait for the rollout, give it 5 minutes of traffic.

    What you should see in Dynatrace: blackjack spans drop to roughly 10%. Now filter your view to status = ERROR: errors dropped to ~10% too. You saved money and lost ~90% of your visibility into the exact requests you would be paged for. Keep that feeling — it is the entire argument for tail sampling.

    Revert it now (delete the two env vars, commit) so blackjack is honest again for the rest of the lab.

    Step 3 — build it: tail sampling on the gateway

    Sampling policy belongs at the aggregation tier where the whole trace is visible. In your Codespace repo, edit observability/collectors/gateway.yaml.

    3a. Drop health checks before buffering

    No reason to buffer spans you will never keep. Add a filter processor:

        processors:
      filter/noise:
        traces:
          span:
            - 'attributes["http.route"] == "/health" or attributes["http.route"] == "/healthz"'

    And put it in the traces pipeline right after k8sattributes.

    3b. Add the tail sampling policy

        processors:
      tail_sampling:
        decision_wait: 10s
        num_traces: 50000
        policies:
          - name: keep-errors
            type: status_code
            status_code:
              status_codes: [ERROR]
          - name: keep-slow
            type: latency
            latency:
              threshold_ms: 500
          - name: baseline
            type: probabilistic
            probabilistic:
              sampling_percentage: 10

    Read it as three sentences: every failed trace is kept; every trace slower than 500 ms is kept; everything else survives at 10%. Those numbers are the cost/fidelity dial you will defend to your platform budget holder.

    3c. Add the spanmetrics connector

    Sampling will delete most traces; RED metrics should not follow them down. Add a connector section (top level of config:, next to processors:):

        connectors:
      spanmetrics:
        histogram:
          explicit:
            buckets: [5ms, 10ms, 25ms, 50ms, 100ms, 250ms, 500ms, 1s, 2.5s, 5s, 10s]
        dimensions:
          - name: http.method
          - name: http.route
        exemplars:
          enabled: true

    Wire both sides — traces export into it, metrics read from it:

        service:
      pipelines:
        traces:
          receivers: [otlp]
          processors: [memory_limiter, k8sattributes, filter/noise, tail_sampling, batch]
          exporters: [otlphttp/dynatrace, spanmetrics]
        metrics:
          receivers: [otlp, spanmetrics]
          processors: [memory_limiter, k8sattributes, transform/drop_player_id, cumulativetodelta, batch]
          exporters: [otlphttp/dynatrace]

    (If you skipped the M3 stretch, your metrics processors simply will not have transform/drop_player_id. Keep the rest of your own file's lists.)

    3d. Validate, apply, verify

    bash observability/validate.sh
kubectl apply -f observability/collectors/gateway.yaml
kubectl -n observability rollout status deploy/otel-gateway-collector
kubectl -n observability logs deploy/otel-gateway-collector --tail=20

    No restart loops, no config-rejected lines in the log — then give it 5 minutes and re-measure in Dynatrace (same window as the baseline):

    • trace count (target: roughly 10–20% of baseline): ______
    • ERROR traces (target: ~unchanged from baseline): ______
    • spanmetrics present: search metrics for traces.span.metrics.calls — RED series per http.route of the game services: yes/no

    What you should see: ingest way down; every error trace still there; RED metric charts that keep showing the full traffic shape — even though most of the corresponding traces no longer exist.

    If errors go missing

    decision_wait: 10s is shorter than some multi-service traces take to complete — spans arriving after the decision miss the vote. Raise it to 15s. And watch collector memory: num_traces buffers decisions; on a small kind node, 50000 is plenty, at fleet scale it is a tuning knob.

    Definition of done — M4

    • Baseline, naive-head, and tail-sampling measurements recorded
    • You can state your policy in one sentence ("all errors, all >500 ms, 10% of the rest") and defend both what it saves (ingest) and what it costs (decision latency, collector memory, blind spots under threshold)
    • spanmetrics RED series visible for game services
    • ERROR visibility ~unchanged vs baseline while total traces dropped
